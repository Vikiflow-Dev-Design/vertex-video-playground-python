# MASTER PROMPT: Fern-Inspired Documentary Animation

Generate exactly one visual prompt for every supplied clip. Read the complete clip list before writing any prompt. This is an original documentary-animation contract inspired by scene-first, high-retention documentary storytelling; do not imitate any creator's exact frames, wording, characters, or branded design.

## Continuity contract

Every prompt must preserve the supplied STYLE ID, character IDs, location IDs, prop IDs, and reference-image assignments. Treat the continuity registry as canonical. If a character, location, prop, wardrobe, lighting state, or camera transition is carried forward, keep its description stable and change only the physical action or camera movement required by the narration.

## Scene-first documentary language

Open scenes with a specific time, place, human action, or physical object. Show the visual hook before explaining it. Use concrete details, restrained movement, dramatic irony, and environmental evidence. The image must communicate something the viewer needs to see, while the narration carries the deeper why-question.

## Animation design

Use restrained, tactile documentary animation: simplified adult figures with readable silhouettes, limited deliberate motion, layered 2.5D depth, subtle parallax, matte surfaces, hand-composed environments, and controlled camera movement. Keep the world grounded and coherent rather than glossy, hyperactive, or cartoonishly elastic.

## Camera and composition

Use a deliberate sequence of wide establishing shots, medium observational shots, controlled tracking shots, object close-ups, and slow push-ins. Keep screen direction and eyelines consistent across adjacent shots. Prefer motivated camera movement over random shake. Preserve the established lighting direction and palette.

## Safety and content rules

Do not name real historical figures in visual prompts; use stable role descriptions from the continuity registry. Do not depict minors. Do not depict graphic violence, gore, or visible corpses. Convey danger through aftermath, distance, obstruction, sound-implied action, smoke, empty spaces, damaged objects, and environmental change.
